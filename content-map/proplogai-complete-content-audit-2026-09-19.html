Executive decision

Repair the system before scaling content production

PropLogAI has a useful topical foundation and healthy crawl paths. The library is not ready for automated publishing because its trust and governance layer is incomplete: source coverage is absent, article semantics are inconsistent, dates and structured data are missing, content types are disconnected, and several changing prop-firm rules are written as universal facts.

Decision: pause new automated publishing. Fix the shared templates and high-risk glossary definitions, rerun the audit, and only then start a small human-approved blog and glossary pilot.

P1 findings

These issues affect trust, correctness, accessibility to search engines, or the safety of future automation. They should be resolved before new content is scaled.

No source layer for important claims

59 of 59 content URLs have zero external sources

Changing prop-firm rules, numeric benchmarks, research claims, and product capabilities appear without named, dated evidence.

Action: create claim and source registers; remove unnecessary general ranges.

Duplicate H1 template

18 of 24 blog pages render two H1s

The newer template outputs the title in the hero and again in the Markdown body. This is a shared rendering defect.

Action: keep the hero H1; begin body content at H2 or introductory text.

Glossary count and sitemap mismatch

36 cards resolve to 35 unique term URLs

“Daily Drawdown Limit” and “Maximum Daily Loss” point to the same URL, which is repeated in the glossary sitemap.

Action: use one canonical page with an in-page synonym unless intent is genuinely distinct.

Inconsistent product naming

“PropLog AI” on 21 pages; “Propol AI” on 7

The approved brand is PropLogAI. The rendered library contains 48 split-name occurrences and 14 typo occurrences.

Action: correct stored content and add a deterministic pre-publication brand lint.

No structured data or date signals

0 JSON-LD pages; 0 dated content pages

No content URL exposes a visible date, a machine-readable publication or modified date, or appropriate article/term schema.

Action: add honest dates, BlogPosting/Article, DefinedTerm, and BreadcrumbList schema.

Blogs and glossary are disconnected

0 blog→glossary and 0 glossary→blog links

Same-type related links work, but readers cannot move from a definition to application or from a guide to a canonical term.

Action: implement a curated definition-to-guide cross-link map.

Source-sensitive claims requiring revision

These examples are not automatically false. They are too broad, changing, or context-dependent to publish as universal facts without current named evidence and clear calculation assumptions.

daily-drawdown-limit

Uses a 4–5% “typical” baseline and instant-breach language, including unrealized losses, without naming a firm or drawdown method.

risk-per-trade

Presents 0.5–2% as a typical prop-firm trader range without data or account-rule context.

prop-firm-challenge

Generalizes 8–10% and 5% targets, a 30-day window, real-capital funding, and pass-rate language.

funded-account

Assumes real capital, gives $25k–$400k account sizes, and 70–90% splits without separating simulated and live structures.

consistency-rule

Calls 30–40% typical and advises readers not to worry if sizing is consistent, despite formula variation.

profit-factor

Calls a value above 1.5 “strong” without sample size, strategy, market, or observation-window context.

loss-aversion

Presents the “roughly twice” effect as a stable fact without a source or qualification.

win-rate

Gives style-based ranges without sources, sample definitions, costs, or risk/reward context.

Recommended execution order

The sequence resolves shared defects first, then the highest-risk content, then information architecture and search presentation.

Fix brand naming and H1 rendering

Repair the shared system and add automated checks before touching individual copy at scale.

Correct the duplicate glossary term

Align labels, unique URLs, the sitemap, and the public glossary count.

Create claim registers

Define allowed product and prop-firm wording, named sources, owners, and review dates.

Rewrite high-risk glossary pages

Make rules variation-first, remove unsupported norms, and add dated first-party examples.

Add dates and structured data

Use honest publication/update dates and validate appropriate Article, DefinedTerm, and breadcrumb schema.

Build cross-type internal links

Connect definitions to approved guides and first-use technical mentions to canonical terms.

Refresh legacy pages and metadata

Resolve overlap with GSC evidence, improve tone, and rewrite snippet outliers around intent.

Re-audit, then pilot automation

Require a clean rerun before a small, human-approved blog and glossary production test.

Method, evidence, and limits

The audit used the live blog index, glossary index, both XML sitemaps, rendered HTML, canonical and metadata extraction, heading checks, structured-data detection, date detection, internal/external link checks, brand-string checks, and manual page-text review. Sitemap discovery returned 62 records and 61 unique pages. Rendered-main word counts are approximate.

Evidence files: live-inventory.csv, live-technical-inventory-2026-09-19.csv, refresh-queue.csv, intent-overlap-map-2026-09-19.md, and the repeatable collector in scripts/collect-live-audit.mjs.

Material limitation

  • Search performance, clicks, impressions, CTR, backlinks, and query ownership were not available.
  • Consolidation and canonical-topic decisions remain editorial hypotheses until authorized Search Console evidence is reviewed.
  • This audit did not edit, publish, delete, redirect, or automate any PropLogAI production content.
  • Prop-firm rules and product feature claims should be rechecked against current first-party sources at remediation time.
