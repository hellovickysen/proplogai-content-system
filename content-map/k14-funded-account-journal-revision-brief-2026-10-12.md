## Fixes to the existing copy

1. Open with a familiar moment: “You are about to take your XAUUSD London-session breakout. Your normal journal has the setup and planned risk. What have you checked for *this account*?” Do not claim every funded account has the same limits, payout conditions or trading-day rule.
2. Teach the extra fields as a small **account card**: firm, program, stage, account/period start, applicable rule page or agreement, checked date and time zone, and the specific restriction relevant to that planned trade. Refresh the card when the firm changes terms or the account changes stage. Do not reproduce a changing rulebook as evergreen article copy.
3. Separate **planned**, **observed** and **officially confirmed**. A journal can record “I checked the firm dashboard before entry” or “needs checking”; it must not declare remaining drawdown, a breach or payout eligibility from a manual note. If status is uncertain, the next action is to check the firm's current terms/dashboard or support, not make a trade decision from our article.
4. Replace the long generic field and starter-template lists with a compact fictional entry showing the difference from the basic journal: XAUUSD, London, breakout, planned risk **$50**, a pre-trade rule-source check, actual net result as a separate field, and one review note. Do not invent a live XAUUSD price, lot size, fixed daily buffer or a named firm's allowance. Say actual loss can exceed planned risk because of execution and costs.
5. Remove the standard five-point discipline score and the prescriptive “stop after two setups” and “reduce size automatically” suggestions. If a process check remains, tie it only to the reader's own written plan, with no universal threshold or automated product claim. Show that a profitable trade may still break the trader's plan, while the firm determines formal account status.
6. Trim product copy to verified, manual journal features in PLAI-002–005. Do not imply a built-in official compliance verdict, automatic rule detection or weekly-summary generator. Recheck current first-party product claims when writing the article.

## Proposed teaching flow

1. Quick answer: a basic journal says what you planned, did and learned; the funded-account add-on says which exact account rules you checked and where that information came from.
2. A short **before-trade card** for the fictional XAUUSD London breakout. Show the plain-language question each extra field answers. A “not checked yet” state must be visible; do not pre-tick a box in a visual.
3. A short **after-trade note** with actual result and behaviour, separate from the firm's account status. Explain in one sentence why the firm's balance/equity and rule calculation may differ from your own note.
4. One weekly reflection: “Which account check did I skip, and what should I verify before the next session?” Point to the existing weekly-review template for wider performance patterns.
5. Close with the distinction between a useful personal record and the firm's binding terms. Link to the main journal guide or the existing [free trading journal](/trading-journal) only where it helps the reader continue.
