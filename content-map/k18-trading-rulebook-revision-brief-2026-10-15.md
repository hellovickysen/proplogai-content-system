## What to fix

1. Open at a decision, not a lecture: XAUUSD reaches a level the trader marked before the London session. The trader's own breakout setup requires a candle close outside that level, but the urge to enter early appears. Explain XAUUSD and breakout in plain language. The example is fictional and teaches rule writing, not a trade signal.
2. Clearly separate **firm constraints** from **your personal rules**. Ask the reader to get the current official terms for their exact firm, program, stage and account, including loss, holding, news and payout conditions where applicable. Copy the relevant constraints into the rulebook in plain words. Do not invent a sample firm's limits or say every breach has the same outcome. Link the existing firm-rules guide for checking the source.
3. Demonstrate one complete transformation: **vague idea → checkable wording → what to record → what to review**. Example: “wait for a good breakout” becomes a fictional trader's named XAUUSD London breakout rule with its own required close, planned stop and session condition. Show the no-confirmation decision in words; do not specify a live entry, direction, target or price.
4. Replace repeated category lists with a compact **one-page core**: source/version of firm terms, allowed market/session and named setup, personal risk/stop condition, what invalidates a trade, and the review note. A trader may keep detail elsewhere; do not claim one page is universally sufficient. Include a blank, copyable template and one filled fictional excerpt so a beginner can see how to complete it.
5. Remove the existing fixed examples of stopping after three trades, pausing after two losses, and a five-part compliance score as if they were general best practice. A trade cap or pause may appear only as a clearly labelled personal example chosen in advance, if it adds value. Record **rule followed?** separately from **net P&L**; a +$ trade outside the rule is still a rule break, and a losing rule-following trade is not automatically a bad process.
6. Make revisions deliberate: record the rule version/date, note the specific repeated problem from journal evidence, change one rule at a planned review, and check the next sample. Do not imply a changed rule retroactively excuses a prior trade. Keep the first-party PropLogAI paragraph limited to recording and reviewing the trader's own entries and approved claims.
7. Remove the unlinked CFTC/FINRA sentence unless exact current primary references are added. Explain any necessary trading term beside its first use and link a reviewed glossary definition. Keep the closing action about the trader's own written process.
