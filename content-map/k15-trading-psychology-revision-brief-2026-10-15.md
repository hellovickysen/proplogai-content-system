## Reader, task and URL

- Reader: you trade XAUUSD around the Asian, London or New York session and already have a setup plan. You sometimes feel pushed to enter early, recover a loss or keep trading after the plan is complete. You want to understand **what changed in your decision**, without being told that having an emotion makes you a bad trader. Write directly to this trader in very simple English. Use USD (`$`), not INR, for forex examples.
- One task: explain trading psychology as the gap between **the plan you wrote before pressure** and **the decision you made under pressure**, then show how to spot and review that gap. This broad pillar should connect its supporting articles, not repeat their detailed routines.

## Fixes to the existing copy

1. Open with one real-feeling but fictional moment: your XAUUSD London breakout plan requires a candle close; after a **−$50** trade, you feel the urge to enter the next move early. Explain in a sentence that XAUUSD is gold quoted in USD and what “breakout” means. Separate **feeling → urge → action → plan check → outcome**. The feeling itself is not a rule break.
2. Replace unsupported “most traders fail,” “same patterns across many trades,” “after two losses execution drops,” and “fastest improvers” claims with modest language. Use the reader's own journal as evidence; do not diagnose a mental-health condition or promise a performance result.
3. Treat revenge trading, FOMO, overconfidence, loss aversion, tilt and boredom as **possible** decision patterns. Give a short plain-English line for each only where it helps the example, then link the relevant glossary definition and detailed guide. Avoid an exhaustive list that turns the pillar into a glossary.
4. Explain that prop-firm rules can add pressure, but vary by firm, program, phase and account date. Remove the universal “real deadline,” mandatory consistency and “one session ends the account” wording. If mentioning a specific rule or breach, check that firm's current official terms and date; this pillar need not teach those formulas.
5. Replace fixed prescriptions such as “pause after two losses” or a universal trade-count cap with **your own written stop rule**. The page can teach how to write a rule before the session and check it later, without telling every trader to use one threshold. A profitable trade that broke the plan is still worth reviewing; a losing trade that followed the plan is not automatically a psychology failure.
6. Narrow PropLogAI copy to verified journal and review capabilities under PLAI-001/002/005. Do not promise detection of named mental states, automatic compliance, real-time prevention or signals. Recheck the first-party product page at article-writing time.

## Proposed teaching flow

1. **Quick answer:** trading psychology matters when a feeling changes what you see, choose or do; compare the decision with the plan you wrote earlier rather than judging it by profit or loss.
2. **Before pressure:** one compact XAUUSD London-session breakout plan: setup, candle-close confirmation, planned size method, and the trader's own stop rule. No live market price or claimed firm threshold.
3. **The moment:** show the same chart situation after a fictional −$50 result. Offer two branches: the next entry still meets the original checks, or the trader enters early because of the urge. Let the reader identify which condition changed. Do not imply every second trade is revenge trading.
4. **Review:** a short five-field journal note: feeling, urge, action, plan match, net result. Show how a later weekly review can reveal a repeated link between a trigger and plan deviation. Small samples are clues, not proof that an emotion caused an outcome.
5. **Next step:** choose one personal rule to test before the next session; route to the specialised articles for emotion tracking, revenge trading and discipline. Close with a supportive, non-clinical note.
