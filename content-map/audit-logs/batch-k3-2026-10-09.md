### Two calculation modes

1. **Forex pip mode**
   - Account balance in USD.
   - Risk input toggle: USD amount or percentage.
   - Stop distance in pips.
   - USD pip value for 1.00 lot, entered or confirmed by the user.
   - Lot step, default visibly editable at `0.01`.
   - Formula: `lots = planned USD loss ÷ (pips × USD pip value for 1.00 lot)`.

2. **Price-distance mode** for XAUUSD and other symbols
   - Account balance in USD.
   - Risk input toggle: USD amount or percentage.
   - Entry price and stop price.
   - Contract size per 1.00 lot.
   - Quote-to-USD conversion input only when needed.
   - Lot step, default visibly editable at `0.01`.
   - Formula: `lots = planned USD loss ÷ (absolute price distance × contract size × conversion)`.

Do not silently assume `$10 per pip`, `100 ounces per XAUUSD lot`, a universal pip definition or a live conversion rate. Examples may prefill only after being labelled fictional and editable.

### Results

- Planned USD loss.
- Stop distance in pips or price units.
- Estimated loss for 1.00 lot.
- Raw calculated lot size.
- Rounded-down lot size based on the entered lot step.
- Estimated loss at the rounded size.
- A compact assumption summary the trader can copy into a journal.

### Validation and boundaries

- Reject empty, non-finite, zero or negative required inputs.
- Reject identical entry and stop prices.
- Risk percentage must be above 0 and no more than 100; show no default recommendation.
- Contract size, pip value and conversion values must be positive.
- Explain that spread, commission, slippage, gaps and the actual fill can change the realised result.
- Explain that margin, open exposure, daily drawdown and overall drawdown are separate checks.
- No live price feed, broker integration, signal, eligibility decision, payout promise or personalised risk instruction.

### Reader-facing example

Use a fictional XAUUSD London-session breakout:

- Account balance: `$10,000`.
- Selected arithmetic input: `1%`, creating `$100` planned loss.
- Entry: `4150`; stop: `4148`; price distance: `$2`.
- Contract size for this fictional example: `100` units per lot.
- Estimated loss for 1.00 lot: `$200`.
- Result: `$100 ÷ $200 = 0.50 lot`.

Every use of `1%` and `100 units` must say it is an example input, not a recommendation or universal contract specification.
