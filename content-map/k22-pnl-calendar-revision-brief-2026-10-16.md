## Reader, task and ownership

- Reader: you open your calendar and see a red day. You may feel you traded badly, or you may want to erase the loss with the next trade. The colour alone cannot tell you whether you followed your plan. Speak to one trader in simple English. India / English is primary; trading amounts use USD (`$`).
- One task: show the reader **how to open one calendar day, check the trade records behind it, and write a short factual note**. A green day can contain a plan break; a red day can contain planned trades. Keep dollar outcome separate from recorded action.

Proposed description: **“See how to review a trading P&L calendar using a fictional XAUUSD week. Check the trades behind each day before judging your plan from profit or loss.”** Verify the rendered title, description, one H1 and self-canonical during implementation.

## What the revision must teach

1. Open with the trader seeing one red calendar tile. Give the answer early: **tap that day, read the underlying trades and your written check, then write what the records show**. Do not call a red day revenge trading or a green day good discipline from colour alone.
2. Explain what a calendar tile can reliably show from logged data: date, recorded daily P&L and perhaps count of trades if available. Say what it cannot know by itself: why an entry was taken, whether a rule was followed, what caused the result, or whether a firm rule was breached. If fees or some trades were not recorded, label the displayed result accordingly.
3. Use one **fictional XAUUSD London-session breakout week**. Keep a single clearly stated written entry check: wait for a candle close beyond the marked level. Suggested daily records: Monday −$40, two trades, both followed the check; Tuesday +$75, three trades, two followed; Wednesday −$110, four trades, two followed; Thursday $0, no trades; Friday +$35, two trades, both followed. Totals: **−$40 recorded P&L, 11 trades, 8 with the check followed**. Verify arithmetic and clarify that fees are not recorded. These values are illustrative, not live trading data or a rule threshold.
4. Walk through the **Wednesday drill-down** in small steps: first see −$110 on the calendar, then open the four records, then read the notes showing which entries did or did not meet the trader's own check. If a note is missing, write `not recorded` rather than assigning blame. An after-loss action can be described only when the sequence is in the journal; the daily number alone does not prove revenge trading.
5. Show one **completed daily review note** with the day's USD result, trade count, observed plan-following, missing data and one next question. A second short contrast can show that Tuesday's +$75 did not make its early entry a good decision. Avoid diagnosing feelings or inferring that the entry caused the result.
6. Remove fixed two-loss/30-minute cooldown advice, the unexplained `2R` example and any universal daily stop. If a trader already has a written stop rule, encourage checking the exact version they used. For prop-firm accounts, the firm, program, stage and dashboard decide official limits and breach status; the calendar is a review aid.
7. Recheck any product statement against current first-party wording and `PLAI-004` before article QA. Say the calendar displays **logged P&L**, not broker-verified profit, automatic revenge detection, automatic rule compliance or a built-in diagnostic verdict. Recheck the existing cortisol paragraph against the primary paper if it is retained; otherwise remove it as unnecessary to this simple lesson.

## Teaching design and SEO

- Suggested flow: **see daily result → open that day → compare actual entries with the written check → mark missing facts → write one next-review question**. Cut the overlapping general journal, weekly-review and discipline-system sections. The [journal pillar](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal) covers the full workflow; [weekly review](/blogs/weekly-trading-review-template) and [monthly review](/blogs/monthly-trading-review-template) cover their own time spans.
- Make the visual the lesson: a handwritten **16:9 WebP cover** with a simple five-day calendar and one circled day; two or three progressive **1:1 WebP notes** showing (1) daily colours and dollars only, (2) the trades behind Wednesday, and (3) the completed note. Put exact numbers and labels under deterministic control, inspect them at thumbnail and 390 px sizes, and enable tap-to-zoom. No forced calculator is needed.
- If a table makes the day-by-day comparison clearer, give it readable fixed-width columns and horizontal scroll inside the article on mobile. The page itself must not overflow. A simple calendar/card layout is also acceptable if it teaches the same facts more clearly.
- Link [revenge trading](/glossary/revenge-trading) only where the term is explained; link [trade review](/glossary/trade-review) at the first useful mention. Optional handoffs: [journal fields](/blogs/trading-journal-fields) for what to record and [performance metrics](/blogs/trading-performance-metrics) for broader numerical interpretation. Verify exact anchors and destinations, and register only links actually used.
