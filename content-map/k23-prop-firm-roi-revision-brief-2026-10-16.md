## What the page must teach

1. Answer immediately: record **received payouts** and the costs for the same chosen set of attempts and period. Do not mistake a funded account balance, passed challenge, requested payout or approved payout for cash received.
2. Define the scope before the formula. Start with paid challenge, reset, activation and renewal fees. Let the reader choose whether to include directly related subscriptions or data costs, and keep that choice consistent across comparisons. Record refunds as reductions to cost only when actually received; show pending refunds and payouts separately. Do not call this audited, after-tax or all-in “real ROI.”
3. Explain in plain language: first subtract received fee refunds from the chosen paid costs, then **ROI % = (payouts received − net counted costs) ÷ net counted costs × 100**. Show net cash result separately as payouts minus net counted costs. If net counted costs are $0, the percent is undefined; do not show infinity or 0% as if meaningful. A positive result means received payouts exceed counted costs for this scope, not that the trader has a reliable edge or a guaranteed future payout.
4. Keep and sharpen the fictional worked example: $150 failed challenge + $150 failed challenge + $75 reset + $200 later challenge = **$575 costs**; $600 received payout; **$25 net cash**; **4.35% ROI** (rounded to two decimals). The third attempt alone would misleadingly ignore the first $375. Make all values clearly illustrative, not current fees or typical returns.
5. Offer a small, accessible **USD calculator/worksheet in the article** because the URL promises a calculator and the live page currently has only a formula. Inputs: total challenge/activation/renewal fees, reset fees, optional related costs, received refunds and received payouts. Show counted cost, net cash and ROI %, with zero-cost handling and clear exclusion of pending amounts. Decide exact treatment of refunds so net costs cannot silently become negative. Client-side calculation only; no saved personal data. Test arithmetic and keyboard/mobile use. The signed-in PropLogAI expense tracker is a separate record-keeping product, not a public ROI calculator.
6. Give one counterexample with a pending $300 payout to show why the result should not rise until that money is received. A brief across-firms example can show why comparing Firm A and Firm B needs the same cost categories and time boundary. Do not turn this into a firm recommendation.
7. Explain what ROI omits: time spent, opportunity cost, taxes, FX and any unrecorded charges. Explain that a small positive percentage does not by itself tell the reader whether to buy another challenge or change account size. The output is a view of recorded cash flows, not advice.
8. Verify the product statement against current first-party copy and `PLAI-006`. Describe only user-entered expenses and payouts and per-firm summary if confirmed. Remove “attached to the specific firm and attempt” unless the current app actually supports that relationship. Do not present the article worksheet as a built-in product feature.
