## Corrections required in the current article

- Replace the unsupported opening claim that **“most traders who fail early challenges”** lack process rather than a strategy. Source-register item RES-007 disallows generic challenge pass/failure rates, and the live page provides no dataset for that causal comparison.
- Do not require fast mental position-size arithmetic. A trader can use a reliable calculator and check the inputs. The meaningful question is whether the **planned dollar risk and account loss limits are understood and checked** before a trade, not whether the maths is memorised.
- Do not require firm limits to be known **“cold”** or “from memory.” Terms can change and vary by product. Require the trader to save or open the exact current official rule page and explain the target, daily/overall loss methods, reset time, trading-day/news/holding restrictions if applicable, fee and account model. FTMO's current [Trading Objectives](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/) show 1-Step and 2-Step rules differ; no FTMO percentage is universal.
- Do not treat support/resistance reading as a universal readiness criterion. That depends on the trader's own setup. Replace it with a check that the trader can explain and record **their own entry, invalidation and exit rules** using their actual method.
- Do not claim that one win streak, a fixed trade count, a checklist score, or a few days of practice establish a reliable edge or a safe purchase. Do not call an unchecked box a failure or a checked box a guarantee.
- The local app stores a stop-loss value and manual rule-following information, but it did not establish automatic stop-move tracking or an automatic stop-loss-adherence percentage. Replace the current product paragraph's “stop-loss adherence tracked trade by trade” and “risk figures calculated consistently” language with approved **manual journal details, notes, rule tags, dashboard metrics and P&L calendar**. A trader compares those records with the original plan and the firm's official terms.
- Remove repeated endings and hard-sell pressure. “Not yet” is a valid output when rules are unclear or records are incomplete; do not promise that a few more trades or a short time period will fix it.

## Proposed trader-facing teaching flow

1. Open with a recognisable moment: you are about to pay for a challenge because recent XAUUSD trades felt good. Before clicking Buy, ask two plain questions: **Do I know this exact program's current rules? Do my recorded trades show how I followed my own plan?**
2. Give a copyable **before-you-pay worksheet** near the top. Fields: firm/program/stage/account and official rule URL/date; loss-limit method and reset time; any extra restrictions; your written setup and planned risk method; a small set of comparable journal records; what you followed or changed; one missing answer; next check. Let the trader write “not checked” or “not recorded” instead of inventing certainty.
3. Explain how to use the official rule page. Confirm the exact program and account; read profit target, daily and overall loss formulas including open P&L where applicable, reset clock/time zone, any minimum-day or consistency condition, and fees/terms. Do **not** duplicate the rules guide's complete calculations. Link it for details. Do not label a condition as a breach unless the current official program says it is.
4. Explain how to use **your own plan and records**. Check one named setup, session and planned dollar risk; compare each recorded decision with the rule that existed at the time. P&L is context, not a readiness score. If the original stop or decision was not recorded, mark it unknown; a later stop-loss field alone does not prove that it was never moved.
5. Fill the worksheet with **one fictional XAUUSD example**. Suggested sample: four London-session breakout trades, with USD results +$80, -$50, +$45, -$60 = **+$15 before any unrecorded costs**. Three entries followed the fictional trader's written confirmation rule; the +$45 winner entered early. The trader also has not checked the chosen program's daily reset time. These observations reveal a process question and a missing firm-rule answer; **four trades cannot establish readiness or an edge**. Every amount and label must be independently checked in implementation.
6. End with a calm decision aid: **rules clear and records available → review the offer's terms yourself; rule unknown → get the official answer; behaviour not recorded → practise and record it**. Do not tell the reader to buy, pass or avoid a specific challenge. If the firm changes its terms, check again before paying.
