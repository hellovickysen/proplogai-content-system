## Proposed trader-facing teaching flow

1. Open with the real moment: it is the weekend, your XAUUSD trades are in the journal, and you remember the $ gain more clearly than the two entries that broke your own London breakout plan. You want to know what actually happened, without calling a profitable week a good process by default.
2. Give the answer immediately: a weekly review is one page with **what you planned, what you recorded, one repeated decision, and one specific thing to check next week**. Show a copyable blank worksheet near the top. A reader must be able to use it without the rest of the article.
3. Walk through the worksheet in plain steps: choose the exact week and account; list the trades and sessions; note USD outcome only as context; compare each trade with the plan that existed before it; pick one supported observation; write a small check for the next week. If a field was not logged, write “not recorded” instead of inventing compliance.
4. Fill in **one complete fictional example**. Suggested records: six XAUUSD London-session breakout trades, all in one clearly labelled week; four followed the trader's written confirmation rule and two entered before it. Show both a winning early trade and a losing rule-following trade so result and process cannot be confused. The exact USD results and totals must be internally consistent, explicitly fictional, and separately checked. Avoid claiming six records reveal a stable edge or cause.
5. End the filled-in review with one cautious observation and one next-week check, such as recording whether the London breakout confirmation candle had closed before each planned entry. This is an example of reviewing **your existing rule**, not advice that every reader should use that entry rule.
6. Briefly explain when a monthly review adds value: compare several completed weekly notes to see whether the same issue recurs. No arbitrary sample threshold, guaranteed habit change or market prediction.
