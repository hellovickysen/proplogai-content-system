## Reader, task and ownership

- Reader: you have finished a month of prop-firm trading and have several weekly notes. You can see the month ended at **+$150** in a fictional example, but you want to know whether your written entry check was followed and which question is worth carrying into next month. Speak to one trader in very simple English. India / English is primary; use USD (`$`) for trading amounts.
- One task: help the reader **turn comparable weekly notes into one short monthly review**. Show what to copy, one completed example, and how to handle missing or too-few records. Treat P&L as a separate result, never a score for discipline.

## What the revision must teach

1. Start with a direct moment: “Your month shows +$150. Did you follow your plan more often, or did the dollar result hide rushed entries?” Explain that the only way to answer is to read the written rule and comparable trade records. Avoid suggesting an XAUUSD trade, price, time or direction.
2. Give a **copyable monthly worksheet** the trader can fill from existing weekly notes: month/account; setup and written check; weeks covered; recorded trade counts; plan-followed counts; recorded USD results and whether fees are included; one repeated observation with the underlying trade dates; missing records; and one question to check next month. This is a suggested review sheet, not a universal firm requirement.
3. Show one **fictional XAUUSD London-session breakout month** using four weekly snapshots. Keep one setup and the same written candle-close check throughout the example. Suggested counts: 5, 5, 6, 4 trades; 4, 3, 5, 2 followed the written check; weekly recorded results +$60, +$100, -$40, +$30. Totals: **20 trades, 14 plan-following entries, +$150**, before any fees absent from the example. Check every sum mechanically. Explain “breakout” and any necessary jargon in plain English, with a useful glossary link if one exists.
4. Mark a repeat observation only when actual trade notes support it, for example one early entry in week 2 and two in week 4. Do not derive the cause from the weekly P&L or treat these few entries as a reliable predictive pattern. If records do not say whether a trade met the planned check, label it **not recorded** and do not force it into the count.
5. Show the filled worksheet after the four snapshots. Separate **what the records show** from **what you still need to check**. A sensible final line is a question for next month's records, such as whether the trader waited for the close required by their own plan on each comparable breakout. Do not prescribe a new setup filter or session restriction from a tiny sample.
6. Remove the current unsupported fixed-percentage trend illustration and language implying that four weeks establish an account-threatening trend. Explain firm rules only as variable terms to verify in the exact program, stage and account; do not infer a breach or payout status from a monthly review.
7. Recheck any PropLogAI feature claim against the current approved product-claim register and first-party interface. Do not promise automatic four-week metric comparisons, compliance verdicts, broker sync, firm-rule verification or a built-in monthly worksheet without direct evidence. Keep the product mention short and factual.

## Teaching design and SEO

- Flow: **open the weekly notes → copy the monthly sheet → compare the same written check across weeks → read dollar results separately → mark what is missing → choose one next-month record question**. Cut repeated “monthly versus weekly” explanations. The [weekly template](/blogs/weekly-trading-review-template) owns one week; this page owns the month built from those notes. The [metrics guide](/blogs/trading-performance-metrics) can explain deeper ratios without forcing them into this beginner sheet.

Two or three progressive **1:1 WebP notes** can show (1) four brief weekly results, (2) plan-followed counts beside USD results, and (3) the completed monthly review with a circled next-month question. Each image must teach one step and remain legible at 390 px; enable tap-to-zoom. A full interactive element is unnecessary. A four-week table is allowed only with readable fixed-width columns and horizontal scrolling inside the article on mobile; the page itself must not overflow.
