# How to Stop Revenge Trading After a Loss

Your XAUUSD stop is hit during the London session. The trade ends at **−$50**.

Price starts moving back towards your entry. You feel the urge to enter again before a fresh breakout candle closes. You may even think about using a bigger size so one win can recover the `$50`.

The danger is not that you want another trade. The danger is that the last loss may have changed your setup, timing, confirmation, size, or reason.

> **Quick answer:** [revenge trading](/glossary/revenge-trading) starts when recovering the previous loss becomes the purpose of the next decision. Stop new entries while you record the facts, apply the loss-response rule you wrote before the session, and only consider another trade if a completely new setup passes the same normal checks. A journal or app can support this review, but it cannot guarantee that the behaviour stops.

## Start with the plan written before the loss

You need a clear reference point. That reference point is your written [trading plan](/glossary/trading-plan), not the feeling you have after the trade.

Imagine your plan says:

- instrument: XAUUSD;
- session: London;
- setup: an Asian-session liquidity sweep followed by a London-session breakout;
- confirmation: the breakout candle must close; and
- size: use the same documented sizing method used for other valid setups.

A **liquidity sweep** here means price briefly moves beyond a marked high or low before returning. A **breakout close** means the candle finishes beyond the level required by this fictional trader's setup. These labels explain the example. They are not instructions to trade XAUUSD or use this setup.

## A planned trade can still lose

The fictional trade follows the written checks and loses **$50**.

That loss does not prove the earlier decision was poor. A planned trade can lose. An unplanned trade can also win.

Now price reverses. The trader wants to enter before a new breakout candle closes. The trader also considers changing the normal size to recover the loss faster.

This is the moment to check what changed:

- **Reason:** Is there a completely new valid setup, or is the goal to recover `$50`?
- **Timing:** Is the decision inside the planned session, or is it an immediate reaction?
- **Setup:** Did a fresh liquidity sweep and breakout appear, or is the old idea being reused?
- **Confirmation:** Did the new candle close as required?
- **Size:** Is the same documented method being used, or did the loss change it?

Another trade after a loss is **not automatically revenge trading**. The order of the results is not enough. Compare the next decision with the plan.

## A valid next trade versus a revenge decision

Use facts you can record. Do not judge the next decision only by whether it wins or loses.

| Check | Still following your plan | The loss may be changing the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | A completely new named setup is present | The old idea is reused without a fresh setup |
| Confirmation | The same required candle close or checklist is complete | You enter early because price is moving |
| Session | The decision remains inside your planned time window | You continue because you do not want to finish with a loss |
| Size | You use the same recorded sizing method | You change size mainly to recover the `$50` |
| Reason | You can explain the entry without referring to the previous result | Your main reason is “make it back” |

This comparison is a [setup-compliance](/glossary/setup-compliance) check. It asks whether the next idea meets the same written conditions as any other trade.

## How to interrupt the revenge-trading cycle

There is no universal cooldown time, maximum number of losses, or position-size rule that fits every trader and every prop-firm program. Use the loss-response rule written before the session and the current official rules for your exact account.

1. **Freeze new entries while you record the facts.** The goal is to stop the next click from happening before the review.
2. **Check the account state.** Confirm open orders and the daily or overall limits that apply to your current firm, program, and account stage.
3. **Label the first trade.** Was it a planned loss, or was a written rule broken?
4. **Ask one simple question.** “Would I take this exact trade if the previous result were `$0`?”
5. **Apply the rule written before the session.** Use your own pause, session limit, and normal sizing method.
6. **Require a completely new valid setup.** If your plan and the firm's official rules allow another trade, it must pass the same normal checks. If it does not, stop.
7. **Review the sequence later.** Record the result, emotion, urge, decision change, and rule adherence separately.

The question about `$0` is useful because it removes the recovery goal from the sentence. It does not decide the trade for you. It helps you see whether the previous loss is changing the reason.

## Revenge trading, tilt, and overtrading are different

These terms can overlap, but they do not mean the same thing.

### Revenge trading

Revenge trading is a specific post-loss pattern. Recovering the previous loss becomes the purpose of the next decision, and one or more written conditions may change.

### Tilt

[Tilt in trading](/glossary/tilt) is a broader state in which you notice that frustration, pressure, or another feeling may be affecting decisions. Tilt can appear after a loss, a missed move, a platform problem, or another stressful moment. It does not automatically mean you revenge traded.

### Overtrading

[Overtrading](/glossary/overtrading) is an observable drift from the planned trading process. It may involve repeated entries, trading outside the planned session, or accepting setups that do not meet the usual conditions. Revenge trading may lead to overtrading, but boredom or FOMO may also lead to it.

The broader guide to [trading psychology for prop-firm traders](/blogs/trading-psychology-prop-firm) connects these patterns. The guide to [overtrading in prop-firm challenges](/blogs/overtrading-prop-firm-challenges) focuses on activity and process drift.

## Can an app stop revenge trading?

**No app can guarantee that you stop revenge trading.**

An app or journal may help you record:

- the trade and result;
- the feeling or urge you noticed;
- whether your own rules were followed;
- the setup, session, and decision reason; and
- screenshots and later review notes.

PropLogAI lets you manually record trade details, notes, emotions, rule adherence, and screenshots. It can analyse patterns in the journal data you enter. It does not provide trading signals, import live broker activity, detect revenge trading in real time, block an order, or guarantee that a behaviour stops.

You can use PropLogAI, a spreadsheet, or a notebook. The useful part is having enough consistent evidence to compare the decision with the plan.

## Copy this short post-loss record

```text
Instrument: XAUUSD
Session: London
Planned setup: Asian liquidity sweep → confirmed breakout
Result: −$50
Feeling or urge: Wanted to recover the loss immediately
What changed in the next idea: Confirmation and size
Would I take it if the previous result were $0? Yes / No
Written loss-response rule applied: Yes / No
Final decision: New valid setup / Stopped for the session
```

This is a short form of [emotion tracking](/glossary/emotion-tracking). If you want reusable fields for every trade, use the [trading journal template](/blogs/trading-journal-template). The detailed guide to [tracking trading emotions](/blogs/tracking-trading-emotions) explains how to record the feeling, urge, decision, and change separately.

During a later [trade review](/glossary/trade-review), read the sequence in this order:

1. What did the plan require?
2. What happened on the first trade?
3. What urge appeared after the result?
4. What changed in the next idea?
5. Did the final decision follow the written loss-response rule?

One record does not prove a repeated pattern. Several records with the same change give you something specific to review. Keep profit or loss in a separate field so a winning rule break does not look disciplined and a losing planned trade does not look undisciplined.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is every trade after a loss revenge trading?

No. A completely new setup may still meet the same written setup, confirmation, session, and sizing checks. Review what changed instead of judging the trade only by its position after a loss.

### Can an app stop revenge trading?

No app can guarantee prevention. A journal may support the process by recording the loss, urge, decision change, and rule adherence. Order blocking or broker enforcement is a separate function and is not a PropLogAI feature.

### Is revenge trading the same as overtrading?

No. Revenge trading is centred on recovering a previous loss. Overtrading describes activity or decisions that drift from the planned process. They can happen together, but they are not identical.

### How long should I wait after a trading loss?

There is no universal wait time. Use the loss-response rule written before the session and check the current official rules for your exact prop-firm program and account stage.

### Does one revenge trade prove a pattern?

No. One trade is one record. Compare several similar post-loss decisions before calling it a repeated pattern.

## Sources and limits

[Prospect theory](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_Tversky_1979_Prospect_theory.pdf) provides general context for how losses may influence choices. Educational material from [Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/controlling-your-trading-emotions/) and [OANDA](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/skills-and-insights/education/trading-psychology/emotions-in-trading/trading-psychology-understanding-your-emotions/) describes emotions as a normal part of trading decisions. These sources do not prove that a particular feeling caused a particular trade or prescribe a universal post-loss routine.

The most useful question after a loss is simple: **“If the last result were `$0`, would this still be the same valid decision?”**
