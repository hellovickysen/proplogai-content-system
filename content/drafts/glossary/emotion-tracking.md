# Emotion Tracking

Emotion tracking means recording a short feeling tag beside a specific trading moment and the observable decision that followed. The feeling is context; it does not prove what caused the result.

## Five simple tags

- **Calm:** able to check the plan without rushing.
- **Hesitant:** delaying a decision the plan already covers.
- **Frustrated:** a recent result still holds your attention.
- **Rushed:** pressure to act before the move continues.
- **Confident:** sure about the decision, while still checking the written rules.

Use your own short words if they are easier. Keep the labels consistent.

## What should you record?

1. **Time and session:** timestamp plus Asian, London or New York session.
2. **Feeling:** one short tag.
3. **Urge:** what you felt like doing.
4. **Planned condition:** the condition from your [trading plan](/glossary/trading-plan).
5. **Observable action:** what you actually did.
6. **Result:** P&L in a separate field.

## Fictional XAUUSD example

During the London session, your planned XAUUSD breakout requires the candle to close before entry. A completed trade ends at **−$35**.

You record **frustrated**, notice the urge to make back the $35 and enter the next setup before the breakout candle closes. The useful record is **frustrated + urge to recover + entered before confirmation**. Do not write “frustration caused the loss.” Use [setup compliance](/glossary/setup-compliance) to compare the action with the planned condition.

## When should you record it?

- **Before entry:** one feeling and the planned condition.
- **During the trade:** any change in urge or trade management.
- **After exit:** the action and result as separate facts.

Keep each entry short enough to use consistently.

## How should you review it?

Use a [trade review](/glossary/trade-review) to compare several similar records. Check setup, session, size, rule adherence and P&L beside the emotion tag. A repeated association gives you a question to investigate; it does not prove cause.

[Tilt](/glossary/tilt), [FOMO](/glossary/fomo) and [revenge trading](/glossary/revenge-trading) are different patterns. Do not apply one of those labels from one feeling tag.

## What should you do next?

On your next reviewed trade, record one feeling and one observable decision. Read the [emotion and decision guide](/blogs/how-emotions-affect-trading-decisions) for one detailed example and the [emotion-tracking guide](/blogs/tracking-trading-emotions) for the complete reusable workflow.

## Sources and scope

- `RES-009` and `RES-010` support the limited statement that emotions may influence decisions.
- `RES-011` supports comparing the action with predefined conditions.
- `RES-022` supports preserving feeling, plan, action and result fields in a journal.
- `RES-024` prevents causal conclusions from journal associations.
- `RES-025` supports the wider trading-plan definition.
- `PLAI-002` and `PLAI-005` restrict product wording to manual records.
