# Consistency Rule

A consistency rule checks whether too much of your total profit came from one day or one trade. Your firm decides the formula, limit and what happens when you are over it.

## First check: best day or best trade?

A best-day rule groups all trades in the firm-defined trading day. A best-trade rule checks one winning trade. They are different calculations.

## Best-day calculation

**Most profitable day ÷ total net profit × 100**

If your best day is $400 and total net profit is $800, the score is **50%**. A 40% example limit means the score is currently above the limit. It does not, by itself, tell you whether the account is breached.

## What happens when you are over the limit?

Read the consequence for your exact firm, program and stage. Some programs delay a payout or require more trading until the percentage falls. Another program may treat it differently. If the rule explicitly allows continued trading, a $400 best day becomes 40% when total net profit reaches $1,000. This is arithmetic, not a profit target or an instruction to force trades.

## Why percentages differ

Named programs can use limits such as 15%, 20%, 30% or 50%, and may use a best-day or best-trade formula. A percentage without its formula, account stage, time window and consequence is incomplete.

## What should you check?

1. Does the firm use your best day, best trade or another measure?
2. What counts in total profit, including losing days and costs?
3. Which account stage and payout period does the rule apply to?
4. Is going over it a breach, delayed payout, higher target or incomplete condition?
5. Does the firm explicitly allow continued trading?
