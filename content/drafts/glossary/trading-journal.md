# Trading Journal

A trading journal records what you planned, what you actually did and what happened. It preserves decision details that profit and loss alone cannot show.

## What is a trading journal?

A trading journal is a record of what you planned, what you actually did and what happened. It can show the setup you saw, the confirmation you waited for and whether you changed the plan.

## Simple XAUUSD journal example

Imagine you are watching XAUUSD during the London session. Your setup is a breakout above the Asian-session high.

1. **Plan:** Wait for price to break the Asian high and for a candle to close above it. Use the stop and size written before entry.
2. **What I did:** Entered after the candle closed, used the planned stop and made no unplanned change.
3. **Result and note:** +$85 after the stated costs. Emotion: calm. Plan followed: yes. Screenshot attached.

Now compare that with a trade that made **+$120** after you entered before the candle closed. The result was profitable, but the record should still say **plan not followed**. Profit does not rewrite the decision.

## What should one entry record?

- **Context:** date, time, timezone, instrument, session, setup and timeframe.
- **Plan:** required conditions, invalidation and planned exit method.
- **Execution:** actual entry, exit, size or planned risk, and any change.
- **Result:** profit, loss or breakeven in USD, with costs stated.
- **Process note:** rule-following, an emotion tag, screenshot and one factual note.

## Why is broker history not enough?

Account history can show the order, entry, exit and result. It cannot always show why you entered or whether the action matched your written conditions. The journal adds that decision context.

## Journal entry vs trade review

A journal entry records evidence from one trade. A trade review checks it against the plan. A weekly or monthly review compares several entries for something repeated.

## What should you do next?

Record your next completed trade using the same three parts: plan, what you did, and result with one factual note.
