# Content Brief: How to Stop Revenge Trading After a Loss

## Primary question and promise

Answer one urgent question in plain English: **How can you recognise and interrupt a revenge decision after a trading loss?**

Show the trader how to compare the next decision with the plan written before the loss. Make clear that another trade after a loss is not automatically revenge trading. The useful test is whether the goal, setup, timing, confirmation, size or reason changed mainly because the trader wants the lost money back.

## Reader problem

Your XAUUSD stop is hit. Price starts moving back. You feel you must enter again before the next candle closes, or increase the size so one win can recover the loss.

The feeling alone does not prove that the next trade is wrong. The danger is that the previous result may have changed the decision process. By the end, the trader should be able to pause, record what changed and decide whether a completely new setup still meets the same written checks.

## Direct opening and quick answer

Use this situation immediately:

> Your XAUUSD stop is hit during the London session. Price moves back toward your entry. You feel the urge to enter again before a fresh breakout candle closes. The danger is not that you want another trade. The danger is that the last loss may have changed your setup, timing, confirmation, size or reason.

The quick answer should say:

> Revenge trading starts when recovering the previous loss becomes the purpose of the next decision. Stop new entries long enough to record the facts, apply the loss-response rule you wrote before the session, and only consider another trade if a completely new setup passes the same normal checks. A journal or app can support this review, but it cannot guarantee that the behaviour stops.

Do not begin with a generic psychology definition, a fear statement or an unsupported statistic.

## Required XAUUSD example

Use one continuous fictional sequence in USD:

1. Before the London session, the trader writes a plan for an Asian-session liquidity sweep followed by a confirmed London-session breakout.
2. The planned trade is taken and the stop is hit for `−$50`.
3. Three minutes later, price reverses. The trader wants to re-enter before a fresh breakout candle closes and considers increasing the planned size to recover the `$50`.
4. Compare the second decision with the original plan:
   - **Reason:** new valid setup, or recover the loss?
   - **Timing:** planned session and confirmation, or immediate reaction?
   - **Setup:** fresh liquidity sweep and breakout, or the old idea reused?
   - **Size:** normal documented method, or changed because of the loss?
5. Explain that the first trade can be a correctly followed plan and still lose. The second trade is not automatically revenge; the decision changes are the evidence to review.

The example teaches a process and does not recommend XAUUSD, a direction, a session or a setup.

## Revenge-specific interruption workflow

Use short, direct steps:

1. **Freeze new entries while you record the facts.** Do not prescribe a universal number of minutes.
2. **Check the account state.** Confirm open orders and the daily or overall limits that apply to the trader's current firm and program.
3. **Label the first trade.** Was it a planned loss or was a written rule broken?
4. **Ask one counterfactual question.** “Would I take this exact trade if the previous result were `$0`?”
5. **Apply the rule written before the session.** Use the trader's own pause, session limit and normal sizing method.
6. **Require a completely new valid setup.** If the plan and official firm rules allow another trade, it must pass the same checks as the first. Otherwise, stop.
7. **Review the sequence later.** Record the result, emotion, urge, decision change and rule adherence without judging discipline from profit or loss alone.

Do not invent a fixed cooldown, maximum loss count, risk percentage or lot size. Do not advise the trader to reduce size unless that condition already exists in the trader's written plan.

## Required distinctions

### A valid next trade versus a revenge decision

Use a three-column comparison: `Check`, `Still following your plan`, `The loss may be changing the decision`.

Rows: setup, confirmation, session, size and reason. Give the table readable minimum column widths and horizontal scrolling inside the table at narrow widths. Do not squeeze the copy into vertical word stacks.

### Revenge trading versus tilt

Explain that tilt is a broader state in which a trader notices that emotion or pressure may be affecting decisions. Revenge trading is a more specific decision pattern centred on recovering a previous loss. Neither label diagnoses the trader.

### Revenge trading versus overtrading

Explain that overtrading concerns repeated entries or trading outside the planned process. Revenge trading can lead to overtrading, but a trader can also overtrade from boredom, FOMO or another reason.

### Can an app stop revenge trading?

Answer directly: **No app can guarantee that you stop revenge trading.**

An app or journal may help you record the trade, emotion, rule adherence, screenshots and repeated patterns. PropLogAI may be described only with the approved manual-journal, emotion-tagging and journal-pattern-analysis language. Do not claim real-time detection, automatic alerts, broker sync, order blocking, live enforcement or guaranteed improvement.
