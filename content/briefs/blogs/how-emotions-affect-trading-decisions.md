# Content Brief: How Emotions Affect Trading Decisions

## Primary question and promise

Help one trader understand the path from a feeling to an urge and then to an observable trading decision. Show how to compare the decision with the written plan without calling emotion a weakness or judging the decision only by profit or loss.

## Reader problem

The reader may notice frustration, fear, confidence, boredom, or FOMO but cannot tell whether the feeling actually changed the trade. The legacy article uses fear-based language, unsupported universal claims, invented performance figures, and prescriptive trade limits.

## Required teaching path

1. Open with a fictional XAUUSD London-session loss and the urge to take another trade.
2. Explain that the feeling is not automatically the mistake; the review asks whether the next decision changed.
3. Establish the written plan: London session, Asian-range liquidity sweep, breakout close, and stable sizing method.
4. Keep process quality separate from the fictional USD result.
5. Show the feeling → urge → decision → change sequence with progressive handwritten panels.
6. Compare fear, frustration, FOMO, confidence, and boredom without making universal causal claims.
7. Give a short record using observable decisions rather than labels such as weak mindset.
8. Link the broad psychology pillar, structured tracking workflow, emotion-tracking definition, FOMO, revenge trading, and overtrading guide.
9. Use the approved PropLogAI emotion-and-rule tagging wording once.
10. Answer beginner questions and state the evidence limits.

## Claim and safety rules

- `RES-009` and `RES-010` support only the limited point that emotions are normal and may influence decisions.
- `PLAI-005` supports emotion tagging and recording whether the trader followed their own rules.
- Do not claim that a feeling caused a result, that one emotion always produces one behaviour, or that removing emotion improves profitability.
- Do not prescribe risk, position size, trade count, loss limits, break duration, a setup, or an entry.
- Keep all fictional forex amounts in USD.
- Keep mental-health diagnosis and treatment outside scope.
