# Content Brief: Overtrading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is overtrading?”** immediately. Explain that overtrading is a change from the trader's written process, such as taking extra entries, extending the session, weakening the setup or changing size. It is not a universal number of trades.

## Required teaching path

1. Keep the two-sentence definition: overtrading is a move away from the process written before the decision, and no universal trade count defines it.
2. Keep the distinction between valid high activity and five observable changes: frequency, session, setup, confirmation and size.
3. Keep one fictional XAUUSD example using IST, the Asian range, London and New York sessions, a liquidity sweep, breakout confirmation and a `−$38` result.
4. Make the test explicit: compare the later entry with the session, setup, confirmation, size and reason written before the first decision. The trade number or P&L alone does not decide it.
5. Explain the difference from revenge trading and FOMO in plain language. A recovery motive may be revenge trading; fear that a move is leaving may be FOMO; either can appear beside overtrading, but the process change is the observable part.
6. End with one action: write down what changed before making the next decision, then review several similar records rather than labelling yourself from one trade.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not define overtrading by a universal daily trade count.
- Do not claim boredom, emotion, a loss or a win caused a later trade. Record the feeling and the decision change as separate facts.
- Do not call every second trade, losing trade or high-frequency strategy overtrading.
- Do not diagnose addiction or promise that a checklist, journal or app will stop the behaviour.
- Keep process quality separate from P&L and do not tell the reader which trade to take.
