# Content Brief: Overconfidence in Trading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is overconfidence in trading?”** immediately. Explain it as confidence that appears beside a decision to change or ignore a rule written before the trade. Keep the definition observable: recent wins or a strong feeling alone do not prove overconfidence.

## Reader problem

The current page treats a winning streak as the cause, says the brain credits skill rather than market conditions, predicts an inevitable losing streak, uses `2x or 3x normal risk`, says previous wins have zero predictive power over the next trade, and claims PropLogAI detects escalating risk and warns the trader. A trader needs a calmer check: place the recent result, original rule and actual decision side by side, then state only what the record shows.

## Required teaching path

1. Define overconfidence in two short sentences using a confidence state plus a changed or ignored written rule.
2. State what it is not: confidence, one winning trade or a winning streak does not by itself prove overconfidence.
3. Use one fictional XAUUSD New York-session breakout example:
   - the trader has recorded several recent wins;
   - the plan requires a candle close and retest before entry;
   - the written size is based on a fixed planned USD loss;
   - the trader enters before the retest and increases size because the setup “looks obvious”;
   - the review may state `recent wins + retest missing + size changed`, without saying the wins caused the decision.
4. Separate three ideas:
   - **Confidence:** the trader feels ready and still follows the written process;
   - **Overconfidence-like process change:** confidence appears beside a relaxed confirmation, session or size rule;
   - **Confirmation bias:** the trader gives more attention to evidence that supports an existing idea.
5. Teach three records: the original condition, the actual action and the reason written at the time.
6. Explain that P&L does not decide whether the written process was followed. A profitable trade can still miss a required condition, and a losing trade can still follow the plan.
7. End with one action: review one decision made after a recent win and write exactly what stayed the same and what changed.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not claim that a winning streak causes overconfidence, that a loss is inevitable or that one feeling proves a bias.
- Do not state that previous wins have a mathematically universal relationship with the next trade. Say only that a past sequence does not guarantee the next result.
- Do not prescribe a risk percentage, lot-size increase, number of wins, cooling-off period or session rule.
- Do not claim PropLogAI detects overconfidence, predicts a loss, compares behaviour automatically or gives real-time warnings.
- Keep the example fictional, in USD and free of live signals.
