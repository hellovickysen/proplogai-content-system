# Content Brief: Trading Journal

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is a trading journal?”** immediately. Explain that it is a record of what a trader planned, what they actually did and what happened. Make it clear that a journal preserves decision evidence that a broker statement or P&L figure cannot show by itself.

## Reader problem

The current page is already useful, but it reads mainly as a field list. It should help a new trader see one complete record in plain English, understand the difference between recording and reviewing, and avoid judging the quality of a decision only from profit or loss.

## Evidence and SEO decision

- Keep `/glossary/trading-journal`; it is the established definition owner and the exact keyword fits the page.
- Semrush India estimated volume 6.6K, global volume 23.0K and KD 48 for `trading journal` on 6 October 2026.
- Useful India variations include `trading journal excel` (880), `trading journal pdf` (720), `how to make trading journal` (140) and `what is a trade journal` (140). These support the cluster, but the glossary should not become a template download page.

- Product wording must stay within the approved manual logging and trader-entered rule/emotion records in `PLAI-002` and `PLAI-005`.

## Required teaching path

1. Define a trading journal in one short paragraph.
2. Separate the record into three simple parts: `Plan`, `What I did`, and `Result and note`.
3. Use one fictional XAUUSD London-session breakout entry:
   - plan: wait for the Asian high to break and for a candle to close above it;
   - actual action: entered after the close, used the planned stop and made no unplanned change;
   - result: `+$85` after stated costs, emotion tag `calm`, rule-followed answer `yes`, and one screenshot.
4. Add a second one-line contrast: a profitable trade that entered early is still recorded as `plan not followed`; profit does not rewrite the decision.
5. Explain the minimum useful fields without producing a huge form: date/time and timezone, instrument, session, setup, planned conditions, actual entry/exit, size or planned risk, result in USD, costs, rule-followed answer, emotion tag, screenshot and one factual note.
6. Explain that a broker history records execution and results, while a journal adds the trader's plan, context and notes.
7. Explain that one entry is evidence from one trade. A trade review compares the entry with the plan; a weekly or monthly review compares multiple entries.
8. Tell the reader to keep labels stable — for example `XAUUSD`, `London`, `breakout` — so later comparisons use the same meaning.
9. End with a practical next step: record the next completed trade using the same three-part structure before adding more fields.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not call journaling proof of discipline, improvement, profitability or future performance.
- Do not infer rule-following from P&L.
- Do not claim automatic broker import or automatic capture; approved PropLogAI logging is manual.
- Keep the fictional forex result in USD and state the cost treatment.
- Do not turn the glossary into a full template or weekly-review guide.
