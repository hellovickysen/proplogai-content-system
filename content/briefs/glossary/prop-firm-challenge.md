# Content Brief: Prop Firm Challenge

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is a prop firm challenge?”** immediately. Explain that it is a named evaluation stage with a program-specific rule sheet. The trader must meet every applicable objective and avoid every applicable breach; reaching the profit target alone may not complete the evaluation.

## Reader problem

The current definition is broadly accurate but still reads like a list of industry facts. A new trader needs a clearer sequence: what the account is, what to copy from the exact rule page, what passing means and why one firm's percentages must never be applied to another firm's program.

## Required teaching path

1. Define a prop firm challenge in two short sentences and say that many online programs use simulated accounts.
2. Explain that `challenge`, `evaluation` and `assessment` may refer to the same broad stage, while the actual number of phases and rules vary by program.
3. Give the trader a five-line rule-sheet check: profit target, daily loss limit, overall loss limit, trading-day or time condition, and prohibited practices or other completion conditions.
4. Use one fictional `$100,000` XAUUSD challenge example. The trader reaches the stated profit target after a New York-session breakout, but must still check every other applicable condition before calling the challenge passed.
5. Add two short named examples, visibly dated `Checked 8 October 2026`:
   - FTMO 2-Step: 10% Challenge target, then 5% Verification target;
   - FundedNext Stellar 2-Step: 8% phase 1 target, then 5% phase 2 target, with no time limit on the checked official page.
6. Explain what may happen after passing: the firm may require another phase, identity checks, an agreement or another account stage. Passing does not by itself prove that the next account uses live capital.
7. End with one action: open the exact current program rules and copy the five checks before placing the first trade.

## Firm and date boundaries

- Do not present the FTMO or FundedNext percentages as normal, typical or universal.
- Do not claim a universal pass rate, fee, duration, payout, account size or consequence.
- Recheck every named rule immediately before implementation or publication if the source-register review date has passed.
- Keep CFD and futures programs separate. A rule from one product must not be applied to another.
- Do not tell the reader which firm to buy or imply that passing guarantees a reward, payout or live-capital account.
