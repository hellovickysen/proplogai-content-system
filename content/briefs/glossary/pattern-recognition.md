# Content Brief: Pattern Recognition

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What does pattern recognition mean in a trading journal?”** immediately. Explain that it is finding the same recorded combination across a defined group of trades. Clarify that this page is about a trader's journal data, not candlestick or chart patterns, and that a repeated combination is a clue to inspect rather than proof of cause.

## Reader problem

The current page turns a few possible associations into hidden performance problems and universal rules. A beginner needs to know how many records were checked, whether the labels stayed consistent, what the numbers actually show and why the original trades must be opened before changing a plan.

## Required teaching path

1. Define a journal-data pattern as a repeated combination found in a named sample of recorded trades.
2. Add one sentence that separates it from chart patterns: chart-pattern recognition studies price shapes; this page studies the trader's own journal records.
3. Reuse the fictional 20-trade XAUUSD sample from Trading Performance Report:
   - 12 breakout and 8 liquidity-sweep records;
   - 4 Asian, 10 London and 6 New York session records;
   - 15 marked `plan followed` and 5 marked `plan not followed` by the trader;
   - 3 of the 5 `plan not followed` records occurred outside the trader's planned session.
4. Explain exactly what that last line means: it is a possible pattern worth checking. It does not prove that the session caused the decision or result.
5. Show a six-step check:
   - define the question, account and date range;
   - use the same labels for instrument, session, setup, emotion and plan adherence;
   - count the records and show the denominator;
   - compare one variable or one simple combination;
   - open the original records, screenshots and notes for missing context;
   - watch future records before changing a written plan.
6. Contrast a useful statement with an overclaim:
   - useful: `3 of 5 plan-not-followed records were outside the planned session`;
   - overclaim: `trading outside the planned session caused the losses`.
7. Explain that small samples, missing trades, inconsistent labels and changing strategies can make a pattern unstable or misleading.
8. State that a pattern does not automatically become a rule. The trader decides whether to investigate it and must keep any personal rule separate from a universal trading rule.
9. End with one action: choose one surprising summary, open every trade behind it and write what the records support in one factual sentence.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not turn correlation into causation.
- Do not label a setup, session or emotion as good or bad from a small sample.
- Do not prescribe universal trade limits, forced breaks or session restrictions.
- Do not say AI finds patterns that are impossible for a person to find or guarantee that a pattern will repeat.
- Do not provide chart signals, forecasts or instructions about what to trade.
- Keep the example fictional and all forex amounts, if any are introduced, in USD.
