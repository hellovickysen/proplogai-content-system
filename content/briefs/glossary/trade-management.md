# Content Brief: Trade Management

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is trade management?”** immediately. Define it as the decisions and actions taken after a position opens and before it is fully closed, compared with the management rules written before entry. Describe common choices without recommending one universal method.

## Reader problem

The current page says active management is more profitable in skilled hands, recommends closing half at 1:1 and moving the stop to breakeven, claims watching a trade makes a suboptimal decision more likely, and cites unnamed prop-firm studies saying set-and-forget outperforms. It also overstates what the PropLogAI AI coach does. A beginner needs a neutral definition, one internally consistent example and a clear line between a planned management action and an improvised change.

## Required teaching path

1. Define trade management in two short sentences.
2. List neutral examples of management decisions:
   - leave the original stop and exit unchanged;
   - move a stop only when a prewritten condition occurs;
   - take a partial exit when the written rule allows it;
   - close the full position;
   - add to a position only if that action and its risk treatment were defined before entry.
3. State that these are categories, not recommendations. The correct comparison is the trader's actual action versus their own prewritten rule and exact account conditions.
4. Use one fictional XAUUSD London-session example connected to J10:
   - setup: breakout and retest of the Asian high;
   - before entry: the trader writes the original stop, one allowed management trigger and `no adding`;
   - after entry: price pulls back before the trigger occurs and the trader moves the stop because they feel afraid of giving back open profit;
   - review: the stop change was a management action, and it did not match the rule written before entry.
5. Explain that the trade may later win or lose; the outcome does not rewrite whether the management rule was followed.
6. Explain three records to keep: original rule, actual timestamped action and reason, and final result including relevant costs.
7. Clarify execution limits: a stop price can be a trigger rather than a guaranteed fill, and instruments, brokers and platforms differ.
8. End with one action: before the next planned trade, write what actions are allowed after entry and what evidence would permit each action.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Remove the claim that active management is more profitable in skilled hands.
- Remove the universal `close half at 1:1 and move stop to breakeven` instruction.
- Remove the claim that watching more causes poorer decisions.
- Remove unnamed study claims that set-and-forget outperforms active management.
- Do not prescribe a stop movement, partial-exit percentage, target, risk percentage, trade count or management method.
- Do not treat a profitable exit as proof of good management or a loss as proof of poor management.
- Do not claim PropLogAI automatically identifies a specific management mistake or tells the trader how to manage an open position.
- Keep the fictional example in USD and clearly label it educational, not a signal.
