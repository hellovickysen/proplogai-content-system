# Content Brief: Consistency Rule

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is a prop-firm consistency rule?”** immediately. Explain that it checks whether one day or one trade makes up too much of total profit. The exact formula, percentage, account stage and consequence come from the trader's current firm rules.

## Reader problem

The current glossary page contains the clearest explanation in this cluster because Vicky manually corrected the confusing points: best day versus best trade, percentages such as 15%, 20%, 30%, 40% and 50%, and the fact that being over the limit is not automatically a breach. J17 must preserve that work, formalise the approved brief and source dates, and keep the glossary shorter than the calculator guide.

## Required teaching path

1. Keep the direct definition and say there is no universal percentage, formula or consequence.
2. Keep the first decision: **Does the firm use the best day, best trade or another measure?** Explain that a daily total and an individual trade are different inputs.
3. Keep the simple best-day formula and `$400 ÷ $800 × 100 = 50%` example.
4. Explain that a score above a 40% example limit describes the calculation only. The rule page decides whether it means a breach, delayed payout, higher target or an incomplete condition.
5. Keep the continued-trading example: if the program explicitly permits continued trading, total profit can rise while the best day stays unchanged. The `$400` best day becomes 40% when total net profit reaches `$1,000`. State clearly that this is arithmetic, not a profit target or instruction to force trades.
6. Keep a short dated table showing that current named programs can use different limits and different best-day or best-trade formulas. Recheck every named program before implementation because firm rules can change.
7. End with the five-item check: measure, total-profit definition, stage and payout window, consequence, and whether continued trading is explicitly allowed.
8. Link the reader to the existing calculator guide for the complete workflow and to the existing tool for best-day or best-trade calculation.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not describe one percentage as the prop-firm standard.
- Do not put a best trade into a best-day calculation or present the two formulas as interchangeable.
- Do not say being over the percentage is automatically a breach. Quote the current consequence for the exact program and stage.
- Do not tell the reader to make a required dollar profit, increase position size, trade more often or take weaker setups to lower the ratio.
- Do not treat a firm example as a recommendation or claim that its rule applies to Indian traders without checking eligibility and the trader's own agreement.
- Mark named-program checks with a visible date and refresh them before publication when the register review date is near.
