# Content Brief: Confirmation Bias in Trading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is confirmation bias in trading?”** immediately. Define it as the tendency to search for, notice or interpret information in a way that supports an existing trade idea. Then show a simple check that keeps the original idea, supporting evidence and conflicting evidence separate.

## Reader problem

The current page says the brain unconsciously filters data, declares that confirmation bias erodes a trader's edge, presents selected indicators as objective truth, instructs every trader to write one bearish factor for every long, and claims PropLogAI objectively compares the plan with execution. A beginner needs one observable XAUUSD example and a review method that does not pretend the page knows the correct market direction.

## Required teaching path

1. Define confirmation bias in two short sentences.
2. Explain that having a directional idea is not automatically confirmation bias. The useful question is whether the trader checked the conditions written before the decision, including what would make the setup invalid.
3. Use one fictional XAUUSD London-session example:
   - initial idea: `XAUUSD may break above the Asian high`;
   - prewritten support: a candle close above the level and the planned retest confirmation;
   - prewritten invalidation: price closes back below the level before entry;
   - actual record: the trader highlights the wick above the high but ignores the missing close and failed retest;
   - review: the recorded entry did not meet the written setup; the final P&L does not decide whether the evidence check was complete.
4. Show a three-column mental model without requiring a wide table: `My idea`, `Evidence required`, `What would invalidate it`.
5. Teach one neutral check: write the invalidation condition before entry, then record whether it appeared. Do not force an arbitrary opposing indicator or opinion.
6. Explain that a chart screenshot can preserve what was visible, but interpretation must still be compared with the words written before entry.
7. End with one action: review one recent trade and write the evidence that supported the idea, the evidence that conflicted with it and which prewritten condition decided whether the setup qualified.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Remove claims that confirmation bias always operates unconsciously, erodes an edge or leads to named trading outcomes.
- Do not claim that one indicator, timeframe or market view is objectively correct.
- Remove the universal instruction to find one bearish factor for every long and vice versa.
- Do not diagnose the trader or claim that the bias caused a profit or loss.
- Do not claim PropLogAI objectively validates the setup, detects confirmation bias or tells the trader which market view is correct.
- Keep the XAUUSD example fictional, in USD and free of live signals.
