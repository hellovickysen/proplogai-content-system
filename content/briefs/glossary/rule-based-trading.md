# Content Brief: Rule-Based Trading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is rule-based trading?”** immediately. Define it as making trading decisions with conditions written before the moment of action and clear enough to check from the record. Explain that a trader may follow rules manually; rule-based does not automatically mean algorithmic or fully automated trading.

## Reader problem

The current page says every decision is predefined, judgment is applied only during strategy creation, rules are always binary and rule-based approaches tend to perform better in prop-firm challenges. Those claims are too broad. A new trader needs to see the difference between a vague idea and an observable rule, how one rule fits inside a trading plan, and why the result cannot rewrite whether the rule was followed.

## Required teaching path

1. Define rule-based trading in two short sentences and clarify that the rules can be followed manually.
2. Show the difference between a vague idea and a checkable condition:
   - vague: `XAUUSD looks strong.`
   - checkable: `During my planned London session, the Asian high has broken, a candle has closed above it, and the retest meets the confirmation written in my plan.`
3. Use the same fictional XAUUSD breakout-and-retest example as Trading Plan. Show the pre-entry sequence:
   1. Is the instrument XAUUSD?
   2. Is it the planned London-session window?
   3. Has the Asian high broken?
   4. Has a candle closed above the level?
   5. Has the planned retest confirmation appeared?
   6. Are the stop and position size set from the written USD risk before entry?
4. State what happens when a required condition is missing: the setup does not qualify under that trader's written rule. This is a process statement, not a forecast about what price will do next.
5. Explain that a rule should be observable enough for the trader to review later. Some rules are yes/no; others need a written definition or acceptable range. Avoid pretending every trading judgment can be reduced to one mechanical switch.
6. Separate the related concepts:
   - **Trading plan:** the whole framework.
   - **Rule-based trading:** using the predefined conditions during decisions.
   - **Setup compliance:** checking afterward whether the recorded trade matched those conditions.
7. Explain why P&L cannot answer the compliance question:
   - a winning early entry can still be `rule not followed`;
   - a losing qualified setup can still be `rule followed`.
8. Explain that changing a rule after entry to make the trade appear valid damages the record. Record what was written before the trade, then review the rule separately after the trade or review period.
9. End with one safe action: replace one vague phrase in the trader's own plan with a condition that can be checked later from a note, timestamp or screenshot.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not claim rule-based trading is superior, more profitable or more suitable for prop-firm challenges.
- Do not claim rules remove emotion, make execution mechanical or eliminate judgment.
- Do not prescribe a specific setup, indicator, timeframe, risk percentage, trade count or session.
- Do not present the fictional XAUUSD sequence as a live strategy or signal.
- Do not infer rule adherence from profit or loss.
- Do not claim PropLogAI defines rules, enforces them or automatically scores discipline.
