# Content Brief: Trade Review

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is a trade review?”** immediately. Explain that it is the check made after a trade: compare the written plan with what actually happened, confirm the recorded result and costs, and write one evidence-based observation.

## Reader problem

The current page jumps from a broad definition to fixed daily, weekly and monthly routines. It also claims automated AI reviews, psychology analysis and action plans without an approved product source. A beginner needs the meaning of one-trade review first, a concrete example and a clear separation between decision quality and financial outcome.

## Evidence and SEO decision

- Keep `/glossary/trade-review`; it is the established concept owner and changing the URL would discard useful cluster links without stronger search evidence.
- Semrush India estimated volume 70 for `trade review`, but its visible variations were mostly unrelated reviews of trading services, companies and an IPO.
- Semrush returned no measurable India data for `post trade review`.
- GSC returned 0 clicks and 0 impressions for queries containing `trade review` during 6 July–3 October 2026.
- Use `trade review` as the primary label because it is concise and established; use `post-trade review` in the definition to disambiguate intent.
- `RES-022` supports documenting decisions and reviewing performance after a trade. `RES-020` supports checking execution and result data against account history.
- Remove unsupported claims about automatic monthly AI reports, psychology analysis, discipline scores and personalised action plans.

## Required teaching path

1. Define a trade review as a post-trade comparison between the plan, the execution and the recorded outcome.
2. State the key rule: a profit does not prove that the plan was followed, and a loss does not prove that the decision was wrong.
3. Use the same fictional XAUUSD London-session breakout from the Trading Journal page.
4. Show a four-step review:
   - `Plan`: wait for the Asian high to break and for a candle to close above it.
   - `Execution`: entered after the close, used the planned stop and made no unplanned change.
   - `Record check`: entry, exit, costs and `+$85` result match the account history.
   - `Observation`: the written conditions and recorded action match; keep this as one example, not proof that the setup will work next time.
5. Add a contrast box: `Entered before the close and made +$120` should be recorded as a profitable rule break, not as plan-followed.
6. Explain the questions for one completed trade: Was the record complete? Did the planned conditions appear? What changed? Did the entry, exit, size and costs match the history? What one fact should be carried into a later multi-trade review?
7. Separate a trade review from a performance report: the trade review checks one decision; the performance report summarises a defined group of trades.
8. Separate a trade review from a weekly or monthly review: those later workflows compare several entries to look for a repeated pattern.
9. Remove universal review durations and schedules. The reader may review after the trade or at a consistent later time, but the page should not prescribe `5 minutes`, `30 minutes` or `1 hour` as standards.
10. End with one simple action: open one completed journal entry and mark `plan matched`, `plan changed` or `record incomplete`, then write one factual sentence.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not label a trade good or bad from P&L alone.
- Do not treat one trade as proof of a pattern, strength, weakness or causal relationship.
- Do not prescribe a universal review schedule, trade rule or action plan.
- Do not claim that PropLogAI automatically reviews trades or generates psychology conclusions without an approved source.
- Keep examples fictional, in USD and internally reconciled.
