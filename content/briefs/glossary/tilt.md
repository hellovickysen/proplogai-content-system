# Content Brief: Tilt in Trading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What does tilt mean in trading?”** immediately. Define it in observable terms: a period when frustration, anger or pressure is present and the trader changes or ignores part of the process written before the decision. Do not diagnose the trader or claim that the feeling caused the result.

## Reader problem

The current page presents tilt as a loss of rational control, lists physical symptoms as if they identify tilt, says the trader knows they cannot stop, prescribes closing every chart as the best action, and claims PropLogAI identifies triggers and recommends countermeasures. A trader needs to know what can actually be checked from a record: the feeling they logged, the original rule and the decision that changed.

## Required teaching path

1. Define tilt in two short sentences using the recorded feeling plus a changed decision.
2. State what tilt is not: one loss, one emotion tag or one imperfect trade does not by itself prove tilt.
3. Use one fictional XAUUSD London-session example:
   - the trader records `frustrated` after a losing breakout attempt;
   - the next considered entry is missing the planned retest confirmation;
   - the trader enters anyway and changes the written USD size rule;
   - the review can state `frustrated + confirmation missing + size changed` without stating that frustration caused the result.
4. Explain the difference:
   - **Tilt:** broad pressure or frustration appears beside a process change;
   - **Revenge trading:** recovering a recent loss becomes the purpose of the next decision;
   - **FOMO:** urgency appears because a move seems to be leaving.
5. Teach three records: feeling tag and time, original rule, actual action and reason.
6. Replace universal recovery advice with the trader's own prewritten pause or no-trade condition. If no condition exists, the next useful step is to write one that is clear enough to check.
7. End with one action: place one pressured decision beside the original plan and describe the change without judging it only by P&L.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Remove the physical-symptom checklist and the statement that a tilted trader knows they cannot stop.
- Do not diagnose a mental-health state or claim that a named emotion caused a trade result.
- Do not say that closing every chart, taking a fixed break or using a fixed daily-loss rule is universally best.
- Do not prescribe a risk percentage, number of losses, break length or session rule.
- Do not claim PropLogAI detects tilt, identifies triggers, warns the trader in real time or recommends countermeasures.
- Keep the example fictional, in USD and free of live signals.
