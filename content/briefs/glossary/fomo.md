# Content Brief: FOMO in Trading

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What does FOMO mean in trading?”** in the first sentence. Help one trader recognise the pressure to chase a move and compare the considered entry with the setup, entry point, session and size written before the urge appeared.

## Reader problem

You may see XAUUSD move quickly during the London session and feel that waiting means losing your chance. The current definition turns that moment into unsupported performance claims: a universal drawdown percentage, invented win rates and an assumed chain of further losses.

The revision should make one point clear: FOMO describes the reason or pressure behind a decision. A winning trade can still be a FOMO entry, and a planned trade can still lose.

## Required teaching path

1. Define FOMO as pressure to enter because a move appears to be leaving without you.
2. Use one XAUUSD London-session example: the trader planned a breakout and retest but considers entering after a large candle before the retest appears.
3. Compare the written plan with the changed decision: entry point, confirmation, stop, size and reason.
4. Explain that the trade result does not decide whether the entry was planned.
5. Give one short pause-and-record check: `What changed between my plan and this entry?`
6. Separate FOMO from revenge trading and overtrading in plain language.
7. Remove every universal drawdown, win-rate, edge, stop-loss and inevitable-spiral claim.

## Firm/program variation and formula assumptions

- Do not assign a universal percentage of daily drawdown to one FOMO trade.
- If the example records a loss, use a clearly fictional USD amount and compare it only with the exact current rule the trader has copied from their own firm.
- Do not say that FOMO always causes wider stops, no edge, more losses or a breach.
- Do not diagnose anxiety or another mental-health condition.
