# Content Brief: Trading Plan

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is a trading plan?”** immediately. Define it as a written framework prepared before trading that says what the trader may trade, when they may act, what conditions must be present, how risk and exits are planned, and what will be recorded afterward. Make it clear that a plan guides decisions; it does not predict the next move or guarantee a result.

## Reader problem

The current page gives a useful component list but leaves a beginner with broad phrases such as strategy, execution rules and behavioural guidelines. It also claims that traders who pass challenges consistently value plan-following more than a perfect plan without a source. A new trader needs one plain-English XAUUSD example and a clear difference between the whole plan, one rule and the short checklist used before entry.

## Required teaching path

1. Define a trading plan in two short sentences.
2. Explain six parts in plain English:
   - market and session;
   - named setup and the conditions that must be present;
   - entry condition;
   - stop, planned USD risk and position-sizing method;
   - exit or trade-management rule;
   - what the trader will record and review.
3. Use one fictional XAUUSD example:
   - market: `XAUUSD`;
   - session: London;
   - setup: breakout and retest of the Asian high;
   - entry: only after a candle closes above the level and the retest meets the trader's written confirmation;
   - risk: calculate size from the trader's chosen USD loss limit and planned stop before entry;
   - no-trade condition: if a required condition is missing, the plan gives no entry;
   - after the trade: record the plan, action, result and whether the rule was followed.
4. Separate three related ideas using compact cards:
   - **Trading plan:** the complete written framework.
   - **Rule:** one checkable condition inside the plan.
   - **Checklist:** the short list used before a specific trade.
5. Explain that a plan can contain judgment. The important point is that the trader writes what must be checked before pressure from the live market changes the decision.
6. State that a winning trade can still break the plan, and a losing trade can still follow it. The result alone does not show whether the decision matched the plan.
7. Explain that account and prop-firm conditions vary. The trader must copy the exact current rule for their firm, program and stage into their own plan rather than rely on a universal percentage or limit.
8. End with one safe action: write one setup in six lines, then check whether each line is clear enough to answer `yes`, `no` or `not applicable` before the next planned trade.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not give a universal risk percentage, daily loss limit, trade count, session or setup.
- Do not claim that a plan removes emotion, produces discipline, improves performance, passes a challenge or guarantees profit.
- Do not treat P&L as proof that the plan was good or followed.
- Do not turn the fictional XAUUSD example into a signal or current market instruction.
- Do not claim PropLogAI creates, enforces or automatically scores a trading plan.
- Keep all fictional forex amounts in USD.
