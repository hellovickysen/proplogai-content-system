# Content Brief: Setup Compliance

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is setup compliance?”** immediately. Define it as the after-trade comparison between what the trader recorded and the setup conditions written before entry. Make clear that it checks the decision record; it does not prove that the setup is profitable or predict the next result.

## Reader problem

The current page treats every criterion as universally binary, uses invented win-rate comparisons to declare that a strategy works, and claims PropLogAI automatically compares compliant and non-compliant performance. A trader needs a smaller and safer question: **Did this recorded trade match the conditions that were written before the entry?**

## Evidence and SEO decision

- Keep `/glossary/setup-compliance`; it is the established definition destination for the journal, discipline, overtrading, revenge-trading, trading-plan and rule-based-trading clusters.
- GSC India, 6 July–4 October 2026: the exact containing-query filter `setup compliance` returned 0 clicks and 0 impressions.
- The same India report showed 23 impressions for `/glossary/overtrading`, confirming that process-adherence language already supports a visible related cluster.
- A fresh Semrush result is unavailable: Servers 1 and 2 reported `Session expired`, and the NoxTools gateway required a new sign-in. Do not invent volume, KD, CPC or intent values.
- `RES-011` supports comparing a considered trade with predefined conditions.
- `RES-022` supports preserving plan adherence and decision context in a trading journal.
- `RES-025` supports treating the trading plan as the wider decision framework.
- Product wording must stay within manual trade details and trader-entered rule-adherence records in `PLAI-002` and `PLAI-005`.

## Required teaching path

1. Define setup compliance in two short sentences.
2. Explain the three records needed for a fair check:
   - the setup conditions written before entry;
   - the actual timestamped decision and evidence;
   - a later comparison using the same wording.
3. Reuse the fictional XAUUSD London-session example from J10:
   - planned setup: breakout and retest of the Asian high;
   - required conditions: XAUUSD, planned London window, Asian-high break, candle close above the level, planned retest confirmation, stop and size set from the written USD loss limit;
   - recorded action: the trader enters after the break but before the candle closes;
   - review result: the required close was not met, so the recorded trade did not fully match this setup.
4. Show three criterion labels: **Met**, **Not met**, and **Not recorded**. Explain that `Not recorded` means the evidence is missing; it should not be silently changed to `Met`.
5. Explain that some conditions are yes/no while others use a written range or definition. The trader must apply the wording that existed before entry.
6. Separate outcome from adherence: a winning trade can be non-compliant, and a losing trade can be compliant.
7. State that one trade cannot prove whether the setup works. Performance evaluation needs a clearly defined sample, consistent labels and stated calculation choices.
8. End with one action: open one recent trade, place the written conditions beside the record, and mark each condition `Met`, `Not met` or `Not recorded` without rewriting the original rule.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Remove the invented 58% and 35% win-rate example.
- Do not say that high compliance proves a strategy works, that low compliance diagnoses a trader, or that a checklist guarantees better decisions.
- Do not require every trader to use the same criteria, session, indicator, risk percentage or trade count.
- Do not turn the fictional XAUUSD example into a current signal or instruction.
- Do not claim PropLogAI automatically verifies setup compliance, diagnoses discipline or compares compliant and non-compliant performance unless that exact current feature is separately approved.
- Keep forex amounts in USD.
