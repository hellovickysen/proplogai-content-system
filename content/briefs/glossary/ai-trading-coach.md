# Content Brief: AI Trading Coach

## Primary question and promise

Answer **“What is an AI trading coach?”** immediately. Define it as software that reviews a trader's own recorded journal data and returns summaries, questions or possible patterns for the trader to check. Make the boundary equally clear: it cannot see missing context, prove why something happened, predict the next market move or decide what the trader should trade.

## Reader problem

The current page overstates personalisation, objectivity, speed and analytical power. It says AI finds insights impossible to derive manually, has no bias and can choose the single change with the biggest impact. It also uses an unapproved product name. A new trader needs a simple example of a useful question, a safe review loop and a clear list of limitations.

## Required teaching path

1. Define an AI trading coach as an assistant for reviewing the trader's own recorded journal data.
2. Explain the input first: date, instrument, session, setup, plan, execution, result, costs, emotion tag, rule-followed answer, note and screenshot — only when the trader recorded them.
3. Use one safe example with the fictional 20-trade XAUUSD sample:
   - useful request: `Show the records marked plan not followed and group them by session.`
   - possible output: `5 records were marked plan not followed; 3 were outside the planned session.`
   - required next step: open those five records and verify the labels, notes and screenshots.
4. Explain what it can help with:
   - organise logged records;
   - group records by consistent labels;
   - calculate or summarise approved metrics from a defined sample;
   - flag possible repeated combinations;
   - suggest factual questions for manual review.
5. Explain what it cannot know from the journal alone:
   - trades that were never recorded;
   - whether a label or result was entered correctly;
   - context that the trader did not write down;
   - whether an association caused the result;
   - what the market will do next;
   - which trade the user should take.
6. Teach a four-step human review loop: `Ask → Check the records → Verify the output → Decide what is useful`.
7. Contrast safe and unsafe prompts:
   - safe: `Compare my recorded London and New York session trades for this date range.`
   - unsafe expectation: `Tell me which session will make money tomorrow.`
8. Explain that `coach` describes the review role; it does not make the tool a licensed adviser, psychologist, broker record or human coach replacement.
9. End with one action: ask one narrow question about a defined set of journal records, then open the trades behind the answer before changing any plan.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Do not claim the AI is unbiased, objective, infallible, faster by a stated amount or superior to a human coach.
- Do not diagnose psychology, infer emotions that were not recorded or call one association a weakness.
- Do not provide signals, forecasts, financial advice or instructions about what to trade.
- Do not claim complete analysis, automatic broker data, guaranteed improvement or profitability.
- Do not treat AI output as proof; require traceability to the original records.
- Keep any fictional forex amounts in USD.
