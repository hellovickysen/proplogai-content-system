# PropLogAI Blog Visual Standard

## Cover directions

Use the approved trading-journal-template cover as the benchmark for premium dark editorial thumbnails. A simple handwritten learning-note cover is also approved when it teaches the topic more naturally and remains clear at thumbnail size.

- Use a premium dark fintech editorial scene with a near-black base.
- Use restrained violet, cyan, and topic-relevant accent colours such as gold for XAUUSD.
- Build the scene around the article's real teaching objects: charts, journal cards, session timelines, checklists, dashboards, calculators, or review flows.
- Create one clear focal composition with depth, polished lighting, crisp interface details, and enough negative space to remain readable at thumbnail size.
- Keep the image educational and analytical rather than promotional.
- Do not place readable generated text, logos, profit figures, money piles, rockets, exaggerated gains, or outcome claims inside the artwork.
- Match the article topic instead of reusing the same chart arrangement mechanically. Preserve the visual family while varying the composition and teaching objects.
- Use a 16:9 cover and verify the crop on desktop and mobile in the existing PropLogAI article layout.
- Deliver and publish blog covers as `.webp`. Keep the approved source artwork outside the public build when a lossless master is needed, and verify the final WebP dimensions, visual quality, file size, and rendered path before release.

For a handwritten cover, use a clean paper or notebook surface, short hand-lettered labels, arrows, highlights, sticky notes, and one simple chart or process. Keep the title short enough to verify every word. Reject generated spelling errors, fake platform details, crowded layouts, or decorative claims.

Keep blog covers at 16:9 because the current blog cards and article hero use that ratio. For text-heavy handwritten images inside an article, prefer 1:1 when it keeps labels larger and easier to read on a phone. Use another ratio only when the teaching layout clearly benefits from it.

## In-article visuals

Use diagrams, comparison tables, annotated examples, handwritten learning notes, and interactive tools when they reduce reading effort or make a process easier to understand. Every visual must have a teaching purpose and the article must remain understandable without JavaScript.

Interactive elements are optional. Do not add one merely to make an article look advanced.

When teaching a chart, setup, or review process with images, reveal it in steps. Start with the situation, add one marked idea per panel, and finish with the complete annotated view. This is easier to follow than opening with one crowded image. Handwritten panels should feel natural and clear, but every label, price, session, and arrow must remain factually consistent.

Detailed teaching images should open into a larger view when clicked or tapped. The zoom view must support keyboard focus, Escape-to-close through the native dialog, a visible Close control, backdrop click, accurate alt text, and a mobile viewport without horizontal overflow.

## Accessibility and safety

- Provide accurate alt text through the article title or a more specific description when needed.
- Keep contrast high enough for labels and controls.
- Avoid visuals that imply a trading signal, forecast, guaranteed performance, account eligibility, or a universal prop-firm rule.
- Use simple visuals to teach. Do not use visual complexity to impress readers, search engines, or reviewers.
