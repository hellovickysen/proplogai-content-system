# Reader-First Teaching

Write for a motivated beginner who may understand basic trading but not prop-firm rule mechanics, journal metrics, risk formulas, or behavioural terminology. Follow `knowledge/12-direct-trader-teaching.md`.

Speak directly to one trader. Use `you` and `your` naturally. Start from a moment the reader may recognise, such as feeling an urge to take another trade, wanting to recover a loss, or finding it hard to wait for a planned setup. Keep the tone calm and practical. Never shame the reader.

Use this order:

1. Answer the main question.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Define the term in plain English.
4. Show a labelled example with assumptions.
5. Explain limitations and firm-specific variation.
6. Give a practical review step.

Aim for Grade 6-8 readability without deleting necessary technical meaning. Prefer common words, short sentences, short paragraphs, and one idea per section. Write so that a motivated non-trader can understand the main lesson.

Avoid trading jargon when a plain phrase works. When a trading term is necessary, explain it in plain English on first useful mention and link that mention to its approved glossary definition when available.

SEO decides intent ownership, metadata, and useful internal links. It must not make the public article sound repetitive, unnatural, or written for a search engine.

Do not assume that a profitable trade followed the plan or that a losing trade broke it. Separate process quality from outcome.
