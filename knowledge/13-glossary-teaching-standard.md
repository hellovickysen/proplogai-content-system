# PropLogAI Glossary Teaching Standard

## Approved reference term

`/glossary/overtrading` is the approved PropLogAI glossary quality benchmark.

Use the same reader-first principles as the blog standard, adapted to definition intent. A glossary page should help one trader understand a term quickly, see it in a real trading situation, avoid a common misunderstanding, and find the deeper practical guide when one exists.

The glossary defines; the blog teaches the full application. Do not turn a glossary page into a second long-form article.

## Definition-first structure

1. Give one complete plain-English definition in the first sentence.
2. Explain why the term matters to the trader.
3. Give one short, clearly labelled example.
4. Explain the most likely confusion or boundary.
5. State firm-specific variation or limits when relevant.
6. Link useful related terms and one deeper guide.

## Talk directly to one trader

- Use `you` and `your` naturally.
- Use very simple English and short paragraphs.
- Start from the definition, then connect it to a moment the reader may recognise.
- Use a concrete instrument, session, setup, journal note, and USD result only when they make the meaning easier to understand.
- Keep IST when local timing matters; do not convert forex or prop-firm P&L examples to INR.
- Explain necessary jargon in the sentence. Link related approved glossary terms without replacing the explanation with a link.
- Keep decision quality separate from whether a trade won or lost.

## Visual teaching

A glossary visual is optional. Use one only when it makes the definition faster or easier to understand.

- Prefer a compact diagram, comparison, or handwritten learning note for a simple idea.
- For a process or chart, reveal one new point per panel and show the complete view last.
- Prefer 1:1 for text-heavy handwritten panels on mobile.
- Make detailed panels clickable or tappable to open an accessible larger view.
- An interactive element is appropriate only when changing a state teaches the meaning better than a static explanation.
- Keep every chart label, session, price, rule, arrow, and example consistent with the definition.

## Final reader test

- Can the reader repeat the meaning after the first sentence?
- Can a motivated non-trader follow the example?
- Is the page clearly different from its deeper blog guide?
- Are related terms explained and linked naturally?
- Does any visual reduce the effort needed to understand the term?
- Are variation, safety, and advice boundaries clear?
